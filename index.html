Extended Project Qualification, 2025 to now

Windowing and the Gibbs phenomenon

To what extent do mathematical windowing techniques resolve the Gibbs phenomenon in the Fourier approximation of non-continuous signals?

Cut a Fourier series off after N terms and it overshoots every jump by about 9% of the jump, however large N gets. I derived the Gibbs constant, then treated each window as a set of weights on the Fourier coefficients and compared how far it cuts the overshoot against how much it blurs the jump.

No window wins on both. The more a window suppresses the overshoot, the wider the transition gets, so the right window depends on what the signal is for.

Mathematics achievement in the UAE's private schools

Understanding Student Mathematics Achievement in Diverse Socio-economic Contexts: A Multi-level Analysis of Private Schools in the United Arab Emirates

Using PISA 2022 data for 10,668 students in 104 UAE private schools, I fitted two-level hierarchical linear models in MLwiN, with all plausible values combined by Rubin's rules and PISA's sampling weights at both levels. About 27% of the variation in maths scores lies between schools.

Maths self-efficacy, immigrant status, maths anxiety and the school's average socio-economic status were the strongest predictors. The socio-economic gradient is steeper for first-generation immigrants, and anxiety hurts more in more advantaged schools.

XLAB research project, SUAT Shenzhen, December 2025

Denoising signals with Fourier transforms

On the XLAB mathematical modelling programme, I built a test signal from 50, 120 and 200 Hz sine waves sampled at 1 kHz, buried it in Gaussian white noise, and cleaned it in the frequency domain: FFT, filter, inverse FFT. I compared low-pass, high-pass and band-pass filters with an adaptive band-pass that sets its band from the peaks it detects.

The adaptive filter gave the highest signal-to-noise ratio and the lowest error. The whole pipeline ran in a few milliseconds per signal, fast enough for real-time use. Next I want to try non-Gaussian noise.

Book

Beyond the Incenter

A Modern Monograph on Olympiad Geometry. First edition, August 2026.

Seven chapters and more than 100 olympiad geometry problems, from the triangle's five centres to cross ratios, radical axes, projective geometry and spiral similarity. Each problem has commentary comparing more than one route, so the reader sees why the clean solution works and the coordinate bash doesn't. I typeset it all, diagrams included, in LaTeX.

At a school of over a thousand students, I found three people who knew Menelaus's theorem.

UKMT markers' reports keep calling geometry "one of the least attempted" questions, and most good books start at training-camp level. This one is the step before: from A-level maths to problems from the BMO, USAMO, China and the IMO Shortlist.

Maths Society magazine

The Legendary 12‑coin Problem: A Linear Algebra Approach

Twelve coins, at most one fake, a balance and three weighings: find the fake and whether it's heavy or light. My article solves it twice, first by minimax casework, then with a 3 × 12 weighing matrix.

Each row is a weighing: −1 puts a coin on the left pan, 1 on the right, 0 leaves it off. If no column is zero, no two columns are equal or opposite, and each row sums to zero, the three readings point at exactly one column. Choose a fake to try it.

The same pooling idea is group testing: during COVID-19, labs combined swabs from several patients in one tube to save tests and time.

Kevinvariant

My free website for Oxbridge admissions tests and olympiads: twelve original TMUA mock sets and six ESAT sets with worked solutions, olympiad mocks and write-ups, a proof marker and a mock interview simulator.

Smart Practice is an adaptive test built on item response theory. It keeps a posterior for the student's ability and serves the most informative question next; a weekly GitHub Action refits question difficulty from anonymous answers. In simulation it reached a standard error of 0.4 in 11 questions, where random practice needed 30.

Cevian

A ranked dojo for contest maths. Problems climb a kyu ladder from 8 kyu (AMC 8) to 1 kyu (the hardest olympiad proofs); the first draft holds 110 of them.

It runs as static files with no backend, so answers are stored as salted hashes rather than plain text. Proof problems get a LaTeX editor and a rubric, and duels run against a timed opponent with an Elo rating.

FingerFlow

with Marc Al Kareh

Upload a photo of a score and your hand span, and FingerFlow suggests fingerings that suit your hands.

Each finger-to-finger move gets a biomechanical cost (key geometry, span scaled to the hand, legato and tempo), and a second-order Viterbi decoder finds the cheapest sequence. The weights are learnt from the PIG dataset of human fingerings. Pianists agree with each other on only 71.4% of notes, so that is the real ceiling.
