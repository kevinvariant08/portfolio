About me

I'm drawn to the moments when intuition is wrong. I expected the sum of ever-smaller fractions to settle down, and the harmonic series diverges.

1 + ½ + (⅓ + ¼) + (⅕ + ⋯ + ⅛) + ⋯
> 1 + ½ + ½ + ½ + ⋯ = ∞

It took me pages of drafts to find the grouping that proves it, and I came away knowing that terms shrinking to zero is necessary but not enough. Saccheri was so sure of Euclid's parallel postulate that he walked away from hyperbolic geometry. That gap between what feels true and what is true is why I want to study mathematics.

I have played the piano since I was three. It taught me to break a hard passage into parts and get each one exactly right, which is how I work through a proof. When a younger pianist with small hands kept asking me for fingerings, I built a model that finds them for any pair of hands instead.

The Gibbs phenomenon

Cut a Fourier series off after N terms and it overshoots every jump by about 9%, however large N gets. Can windowing fix that?

≈ 9%overshoot that never shrinks as N grows
3windows compared, from rectangular to Hann

  2. Gibbs constant
  3. Windows as weights
  4. Overshoot vs width

+ The full story

My question: to what extent do mathematical windowing techniques resolve the Gibbs phenomenon in the Fourier approximation of non-continuous signals?

I derived the Gibbs constant with help from an undergraduate thesis, then treated each window as a sequence of weights on the Fourier coefficients and measured two things: the overshoot, and how wide the jump becomes.

No window wins on both. The more a window suppresses the overshoot, the wider the transition gets, so the right window depends on what the signal is for.

Maths achievement in UAE private schools

A multilevel analysis of PISA 2022: what separates students, and what separates schools?

+ The full story

Dubai's private schools average 497 in PISA maths, among the highest-scoring regions in the world, while the UAE as a whole averages 431. I wanted to know what drives scores inside that system.

I fitted five two-level hierarchical linear models in MLwiN, combining all plausible values with Rubin's rules and using PISA's sampling weights at both levels.

Maths self-efficacy, immigrant status, maths anxiety and the school's average socio-economic status were the strongest predictors. The socio-economic gradient is steeper for first-generation immigrants, and anxiety hurts more in more advantaged schools.

The legendary 12‑coin problem

Twelve coins, at most one fake, a balance and three weighings. My article solves it twice: by minimax casework, then with a 3 × 12 matrix.

Each row is a weighing: −1 is the left pan, 1 the right, 0 off the scale. If no column is zero, no two columns are equal or opposite, and each row sums to zero, the three readings point at exactly one coin. Pick a fake.

+ How Smart Practice works

Smart Practice is an adaptive test built on item response theory. In the browser it keeps a posterior for the student's ability on a grid and serves the most informative question next.

Every week a GitHub Action refits question difficulty from anonymous answers by Bayes-modal EM and opens a pull request. In simulation, adaptive selection reached a standard error of 0.4 in 11 questions, where random practice needed 30.

The site also has a proof marker, a mock Oxbridge interview simulator, a Trap Map that names the mistake behind each wrong option, and duels.

FingerFlow

Upload a photo of a score and your hand span, and FingerFlow suggests piano fingerings that suit your hands.

71.4%how often human pianists agree: the real ceiling

Each finger-to-finger move gets a biomechanical cost (key geometry, span scaled to the hand, legato, tempo), and a second-order Viterbi decoder finds the cheapest sequence. Weights are learnt from the PIG dataset of human fingerings.
